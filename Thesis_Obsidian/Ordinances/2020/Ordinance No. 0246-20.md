To constitute valid ordinance, it: 1) must not contravene the
Constitution or any statute; 2) must not be unfair or oppressive; 3) must not
be partial or discriminatory; 4) must not prohlbit but may regulate trade; 5) must
not be unreasonable; and 6) must be general and consistent with public policy.
The subject ordinance, which penalizes the mere "failure to offer the
lowest available floor room accommodation" to senior citizens, etc. is too
sweeping that even if there is a valid reason not to offer the hotel owner has to
make an offer.
It is untruthful and absurd to make an offer knowing that there is nothing
to offer because all such accommodations are occupied. It makes no sense, and
yet, the ordinance requires to make an offer, lest, the hotel owner will be
penalized.
The penalty of suspension for first offense is vague, oppressive and
unreasonable. As observed, the ordinance failed to fix the maximum number of
days the business permit is suspended. It gives the implementing agency the
uncontrolled discretion on the suspension of business permit. Nothing can deter
if it chooses to impose a month or year suspension.
In view thereof, this office recommends veto of the subject ordinance.

SECTION 2.DECLARATION OF POLICY- It is the declared policy of the State
to provide assistance and protect the welfare of pregnant women, senior citizens and
Persons With Disabilities (PWDs) because of their fragile condition which requires the
understanding and assistance of the society which they are part of. Towards this end, it
is necessary to ensure their safety and protection by reserving for them the lowest
available floor accommodation of every hotel and resort of this City, especially nowadays
where there have been rampant occurrences of natural calamities such as eafthquakes
and floods.
Article XIII, SECTION 11 of the Constitution, provides that the State shall adopt an
integrated and comprehensive approach to health development which shall endeavor to
make essential goods, health and other social services available to all the people at
affordable cost. There shall be priority for the needs of the underprivileged, sick, elderly,
disabled, women and children. Article XV, SECTION 4 of the Constitution, fufther, declares
that it is the duty of the family to take care of its elderly members while the State may
design programs of social security for them.
This Ordinance seeks to cater the special needs of the aforementioned persons
especially in the case of emergencies wherein their safety would be in peril because of
their physical restrictions compared to the normal ones.
