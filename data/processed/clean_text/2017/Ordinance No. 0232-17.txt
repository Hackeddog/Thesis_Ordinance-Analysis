EXHIBIT A
Starkey Hearing Foundation's Multi-Phase Approach
The Starkey Hearing Foundation utilizes a continuous action model to developing sustainable
community-based systems of hearing care, The first step is to identify strong local partners
such as local NGOs, schools, government ministries, hospitals, faith-based organizations, and
service organizations who are willing to collaborate to create a sustainable community-based
system of hearing care in their community, city or country, as well as a talented local
coordinator with a background as a nurse, social worker, or teacher to be a point of contact for
all parties and to be responsible tor assisting in the plannlng and preparation of the Phase 2
Hearing Mission and conducting the Phase 3 AfterCare Program.
Phase 1- Patient ldentiflcation
Phase 1 engages appropriate partners and media outlets in an outreach effort to identify
potential patients in the catchment area and then register and screen them and make ear
impressions from the appropriate pataents when applicable.
o ldentiff Paftners
Through our community-based hearing health model we work to empower local residents to
create ownership over local hearing health services. We do this by partnering with schools and
NGOs to promote hearing health awareness, identify patients in need of hearing health services
and sustain proper use of hearing devices. We recruit and train local volunteers and
coordinators, so stakeholders on the ground are able to ensure short and long term access to
hearing health services for current and prospective patients.
o Patient Outreach
Current media outlets such as radio announcements, newspaper, word of mouth, service
organizations, faith-based organizations, NGOs, Schools for the Deaf, Hospitals and
Government Ministries serve as the best way to conduct patient outreach and mobilization
about our Phase 1 missions.

o Screen for Hearing Loss
Prospective patients are screened by local health professionals via voice test to determine if
they are candidates for hearing aids.
o Take Ear lmpressions (when applicable)
Trained individuals take custom ear impressions of the patients to send back to Starkey
Headquarters in the USA to be made into custom earmolds. ln other instances, patients will be
fit with specially designed stock earmolds.
Phase 2 - Hearing Mission (Hearing Aid Distribution)
Within a year after Phase 1, Foundation representatives return to the location and provide
earmolds, hearing aids, and a supply of batteries, all at no cost, for the screened patients,
usually consulting with 300 to 5@ patients per day. Facilities required include tables, chairs,
electricity, and toilets, as well as tents (depending on weather) and possiblV other
infrastructure.
o Fit Hearing Aids with Earmolds
Patients are contacted beforehand and come to the Phase 2 Hearing Mission registration
station to confirm their contact details, pass screening by health professionals, receive their
earmolds and receive individual fitting of hearing aids using the WFA Method, whereby the
most comfortable level (MCL) of hearing for the patient is identified.
o Train, Educate and Counsel Patients
After the patients are fit with hearing aids, they are given batteries and a desiccant pouch for
protection of the hearing aids and are then taught by local volunteers how to operate and care
for their hearing aids and are given a basic response test to confirm that they understand the
principles of both operation and care. The patients are then provided with information on
where to go and who to contact for follow-up services ("AfterCare").

Phase 3 - AfterCare (Patient Follow-Up)
Phase 3 is designed to ensure that the hearing aids provided can be used well into the future.
o Provide On-Going Patient Education and Training
Patients have access to a monthly AfterCare Service Center that is generally held at a
Iocal/national hospital, school for the deaf, or the office of a service organization in the capital
of a country. ln many countries there are regional AfterCare Service Centers as well. Patients
also have access to an AfterCare phone number that is held by a local coordinator who can help
troubleshoot or re-counsel the patients via SMS/text or over the phone. lf the problem cannot
be solved over the phone, patients are then recommended to come see the local coordinator in
person at the AfterCare Servrce Center. For Srudents at schools for the deaf who receive
hearing aids, our teams of coordinators conduct bi-annual school visits to ensure the students
are wearing their hearing aids and to troubleshoot any problems they may have as well as to
conduct parent/teacher trainings on hearing aids and hearing health awareness and
prevention. A supply of batteries is provided to the service centers.
