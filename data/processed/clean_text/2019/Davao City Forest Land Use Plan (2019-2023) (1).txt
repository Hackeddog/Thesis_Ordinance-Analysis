1.0 Background
Davao City covers a total land area of 244,000 hectares comprising 105,961.95
hectares of Alienable and Disposable Lands or A & D (43.43%), 126,933.87
hectares of Timberland (52.02%) and 11 ,104.18 hectares of Mt. Apo Natural Park
or MANP (4.55o/o). lts jurisdiction comprises a total of 182 barangays. About 47
(25.82%) barangays with forestland are subject for Forest Land Use Plan (FLUP).
Davao City is located in the southeastern part of Mindanao. lt is bounded on the
north by Bukidnon; on the east partly by Davao del Norte and Davao Gulf; on the
south by Davao del Sur; and on the west by North Cotabato. Davao City Proper is
approximately 946 aerial kilometers or 588 statute miles, southeast of Manila.
A substantial part of Davao City is mountainous, characterized by extensive
mountain ranges with uneven distribution of plateaus and lowlands. The mountain
range which delimits the western boundary of the city extends as far down to South
Cotabato. This mountain range nurses the highest peak in the Philippines, which
is Mt. Apo located at the boundaries of North Cotabato, Davao del Sur and Davao
City. It/t. Apo has an elevation of about 10,311 feet (3,144 meters) above sea level.
It has been considered as a semi-active volcano.
Forest areas cover about 18,571.47 hectares or 7.61 percent of the city's total land
area. These are those covered with woody type of vegetation whether natural or
planted forest. This natural forest is most extensive in Baguio, Marilog, and
Paquibato (Figure 1)
The DENR has identified and delineated areas classified as Forestland or
Timberland which covers 126,933.87 hectares. There are 105,961.95 hectares
classified as Alienable and Disposable.
Agro-forestry is now being intensified with permanent multi-crops to increase
agriculture productivity in the uplands as well as promote watershed development.
Recommended agro forestry products include - forest trees, high valued fruit trees
and high valued agricultural crops.
Davao City's vision is to become a "Premier Socio-economic, lnvestment, Tourism
Center in tt/indanao, East ASEAN Growth Area (EAGA) and the Asia-Pacific
Region propelled by enlightened leaders and empowered citizenry and committed
to sustainable social growth and development, and economic growth without
compromising the environment under the guidance of Divine Providence."
However, forest especially production forest species continue to be harvested by
communities for their use. Some of the tree species are indiscriminately harvested
to make charcoal to be sold in the lowlands. Prioritizing biodiversity has always
been a challenge thus reforestation projects often only include one or two species

The protection of environmental assets in forestland and conservation areas of the
city is essential. A healthy ecology and rich biodiversity not only achieve
environmental protection in their own right, but also create a more liveable city,
which strengthens the city's competitive advantage. Climate change will severely
affect the health of the natural environment. lt is critical to intervene now to protect
and enhance the city's environmental assets so that they are more resilient to the
risks of climate change. Areas of high environmental significance, protected public
lands -Mt. Apo Natural Parks and Watershed areas. Should be protected from
development unless a specific regulatory exemption applies.
The Davao City Forest Land Use Plan provides a framework for resource
management and mitigating measures in forest land protection and conservation.
The formulation of the FLUP is prepared with the principles of transparency,
participatory and inclusive with the various stakeholders at the community.
The local government units have the major role in the management of forest and
forest lands. lt is also their role to implement programs and projects that could
address the issues and problems at the community level in a sustainable manner.
With the vision of the city for economic growth without compromising the
environment and natural resources, it is important to have a framework and a
roadmap that will guide all actions and initiatives of the local government units for
more rational utilization and management of the city's forest resource.
Thus, the formulation of Forest Land Use Plan is of immediate concern. This will
integrate the forest management, land use and the ancestral domain sustainable
development protection plan. This is also a tool that promotes stable and selfsufficient communities through a sustainable environment and institutionalized
participatory management and improving the socio-economic conditions of
indigenous people and other people occupying forest lands.
2.0 Vision, Mission, Goals and Objectives on Forests and Forest Lands
2.1 Vision
A vibrant forestland sustained by an empowered community under a dynamic
leadership for the protection and conservation of Mother Nature.

2.2 Mission
Conservation, protection and restoration of the forest and forestland by
empowered stakeholders for the general welfare of the people without
compromising its culture
2.3 Goals and Objectives
1. To enhance and sustain the existing forest cover
a. To create and strengthen "bantay bukid" and "bantay dagat" at barangay
level
b. To intensify the implementation of National and local Greening Programs
c. To conserve and manage coastal resources effectively and efficiently
d. To rehabilitate and conserve the forest, forestland and riparian zone
2. To promote and instill the awareness on forest conservation and protection
a. To expand advocacy activities, IEC to communities and stakeholders
within forestlands
b. To encourage cooperation among stakeholders, vigilance in the
management of environment, and active participation in the conservation
and protection of watersheds
3. To promote agroforestry in Forest and Forestland
a. To capacitate farmers in sustainable agroforestry farming
b. To increase awareness in agroforestry
c. To explore agroforestry as potential investment area
4. To improve the quality of life of the communities in the forest and forestland
a. To provide sustainable livelihood programs to improve living conditions of
communities
b. To ensure availability of basic utilities or services
c. To ensure safety of communities
5. To strengthen the convergence of the stakeholders in the management of the
forestland
a. To strengthen people's organizations (PO) annually through implementing
programs that promote forest conservation, protection and management
b. To sustain coordination between national and local government agencies
c. To heighten implementation of national and local laws for the preservation,
protection and sustainability of forest and forest land

6. To develop ecotourism and nature based tourism potentials in Forest and
Forestland
a. To conduct inventory, assessment, and declare the potential eco-tourism
and nature based tourism area
b. To develop and protect the potential eco-tourism sites

Climate/Rainfall
Davao City enjoys a mild tropical climate. Compared with other parts of the
Philippines in which there is a distinct hot and wet season. lt enjoys the privilege
of a climate where the days are always sunshiny and mild followed by nights of
rain. The city is outside the typhoon belt and lacks major seasonal variations.
The annual average minimum and maximum temperature in 2010 is 24.5
and 32.3 degrees Celsius respectively. However, the 30-year trend (1981-2010)
shows that the annual average minimum temperature increased by almost 3
degrees while the annual average maximum temperature decreased by 2 degrees.
The average annual rainfall has a recorded increase by 1Omm from 1990
to 2010. Although a remarkable increase was observed in 1994, it has remained
in the 150mm level.
The predominant wind direction is northward from the Davao Gulf where the
cooler air of the sea replaces the warm air mass over the city. A surrounding chain
of mountains protect the city effectively from strong winds.

Hydro-Geology
Davao City is blessed with abundant fresh drinking water both ground and
surface. Based on the terrain analysis conducted by the ft/ines and Geosciences
Bureau (lVlGB) Xl, there are different types of aquifer depending on the depth.
Perched aquifers occur at 1-3 meters deep while those that occur at 5-25 meters
are shallow aquifers. Moderately deep aquifers occur at25-100 meters while very
deep aquifers lie at depths greater than 100 meters The very deep aquifers are
recharged along deep river channels and cracks along large river systems in
Baguio, Calinan, Tugbok and Toril Districts.
There are eight major watersheds that traverse Davao City. The
southernmost is the Sibulan River Watershed having its headwaters in Davao City
and draining towards the municipality of Sta. Cruz. Lipadas River Watershed cuts
across from the slopes of tVlt. Apo draining towards Lizada. Talomo Watershed has
its headwaters in Mt. Talomo traversing parts of Calinan and Tugbok Districts
flowing down to Barangay Talomo. The smallest major watershed is the Matina
Pangi River Watershed which runs from Biao Escuela draining towards
MatinaAplaya. The largest is Davao City Watershed which ironically has its
headwaters in Bukidnon, covering parts of tvlarilog and Paquibato districts
funneling down towards Poblacion District. Lasang River Watershed covers rivers
that flow from Paquibato district entering Davao del Norte and draining back to
Bunawan district. Tuganay River Watershed is almost totally part of Davao del
Norte except the middle portion that enters Paquibato District. Bunawan River
traverse from Barangay Fatima draining to Barangay Bunawan.
The terrain analysis conducted by the [\Iines and Geosciences Bureau have
classified the various land use capability units as a result of the comprehensive
assessment of the geophysical environment of the watersheds by rating the

various geologic factors such as slope, lithology, geomorphology, soils and
hazards.

Land Use Capability and Environmental Management Classification
9.1 Agricultural Land Use Capable Areas
The agricultural land-use capable areas are divided into three classes,
namely the agricultural non-tillage areas, agricultural tillage areas, and
agricultural areas for rice, corn and/or fishpond.
Agricultural - Non-Tillage / Agroforestry
These are areas that, due to environmental constraints and significance
to adjacent areas, should not be tilled. Such areas should at best be developed
as agro-forest areas. This type of land use is recommended for areas
delineated with a moderate to high susceptibility to mass movement and
erosion.
Agricultura! - Ti!lage (a!! types of agricultura! practice)
These areas have minimal environmental constraints and therefore
could be devoted for agricultural developments that require intensive soil
preparation. However, development in such areas requires strict soil
conservation and management program and best industry practices should be
applied.
Agricultural - Rice / Corn / Fishpond
Areas identified as agricultural area for rice, corn and fishponds are the
fluvio-alluvial plains that are occasionally flooded and/or water logged thus
compatible for said land-use as they are tolerant of excessive water.
9.2 Conservation Areas
These areas are delineated as such due to serious environmental
constraints and their extreme importance to maintaining environmental
balance. Said areas are either highly prone to mass movements, important to
maintaining surface water supply, or are important recharge areas for
groundwater. lt is therefore necessary to manage the development therein to
conform to the conservation/protection objectives. lncluded in the conservation
area is the ft/t. Apo National Park as delineated in Republic Act # 9237
approved on February 3,2004.

9.3 Resource Conservation
These areas are delineated as such due to the relative importance of
the resources therein to human development activities. These are parts of the
city wherein substantial deposits of limestone that could be used in the
production of agricultural- lime, industrial-lime, cement and/or building blocks
for agricultural, industrial, and construction industries respectively.

9.0 Key Findings
9.1 Problems and lssues
Numerous issues were identified by the stakeholders throughout the conducted
community mapping and group discussions. Basing from the results of situational
analysis, kaingin and timber poaching, unsound farming system, rampant selling of
land rights of lPs to migrants, conversion of forestlands to agricultural use,
undeveloped potential nature-based tourism, high cost of production on high value
crops, insufficient agriculture and infrastructure support facilities and presence of
armed group resulting to a challenged peace and order situation in some of the upland
barangays are the main known concerns.
The threats to the forestlands of the city are recurring and intertwined and are
mostly by anthropogenic causes. Particular problems are outcome of weak
implementation of laws and lack of regulation, but predominantly are based on the
tremendous poverty-related pressure of the people. lnequity on land access and
access to other basic services forced people to occupy the protected areas of
forestlands to survive. Given the severe ecosystems changes in the forestlands, the
challenge for rehabilitation enormous task. Ecosystem alterations are mainly invasive
)

and environmental restoration may not be sufficient to restore the area, if not to its
ideal condition, but at least better than what is currently in situation.
More importantly, finding the balance between the economics of the
communities apropos ecological soundness, which can be efficiently attained by
empowering the stakeholders, establishing working procedures on law
implementation, and finding sustainable alternative livelihood that will reduce the used
of forest products and lessen the pressure on the forest and forestlands.

d) Unregulated Land Uses. Steep slopes are used for settlements regardless of
danger posed by landslides.
e) Rampant selling of rights of lPs to prone to encroachment and unregulated

f) Socio-economic lssues

10.2 Technical Strategies
Protection and rehabilitation of existing natural forests
a
Protection of existing natural forests shall be enforced through the
establishment of check-points both in entrance and exit, establishment of look-out
tower, deputization of forest guards (AD guards), mobilization of anti-timber
poaching task force, strengthening of monitoring, implementation of multi-sectoral
forest protection, strengthen the enforcement of mining laws. Reforestation
projects in partnership with DENR, conduct regular inspection and monitoring on
tenure holders, conduct of Communication, Education and Public Awareness
(CEPA) within the upland barangays and capacitate the lP and key stakeholders
on the protection of natural resources. With these, issues on timber poaching and
undocumented mining will be addressed thus improving the forest cover.
Slash and burn practices and agriculture activities shall be prohibited within
the protection zone while planting of indigenous species/ forest trees is
encouraged.

c.

measures and construction of gabions, strengthen the implementation of
watershed management, inventory of potential sources of potable water, approval
of the water sufficiency code and ordinance banning informal settlers and
strengthen the implementation of NO-BUILD policy within environmentally critical
areas and along river banks, strengthen the implementation of Ecological Solid
Waste lVlanagement (ESWI\/) ordinance by conducting regular monitoring and site
inspection, construction of Material Recovery Facilities (t\4RF), development of a
sanitary landfill, giving recognition to the awards of best implementer on solid
waste management in the barangay level and granting of incentives to individuals
who will report violators of ESWIT/ ordinance.
d.
Nature based tourism development
Potential tourism sites such as caves and waterfalls shall be assessed,
classified and developed for eco-tourism in partnership with Department of
Tourism. Cave and Waterfalls Development lt/anagement Plan shall also be put in
place to ensure sustainability and to establish guiding policies in the operation and
management of these eco-tourism sites. Likewise, identified waterfalls which are
potential for hydropower development shall be assessed for feasibility study.
However, farm tourism of agro forest plantation is also considered for tourism
development.

e.
Development of the priority sub-watershed
Eight (8) sub-watershed is identified as the priority watershed for
development by providing appropriate programs, projects and activities (PPAs)
towards forest and forestland management, strengthened institutional capacity to
manage forest resources, improved implementation and adoption of sustainable
forest resource management and improved facilities and support services to the
communities. However, other sub-watersheds are also considered by providing
appropriate interventions tailored to its pressing needs.

Monitoring and Evaluation (RBIVE) framework shall be used to give focus on the
measurement of results that the relevant stakeholders expect to achieve in the
implementation of the plan. RBIUE is a management tool designed to measure how well
the involved players are performing based on the results expected from the
implementation of the plan. Assessing outcomes based on agreed time frame is
emphasized under RBtVlE. Stakeholders or players in the FLUP were identified and their
respective roles are define in the Stakeholder's Analysis.
The evaluation mechanism shall involve an assessment of a planned, ongoing or
completed intervention to determine its relevance, efficiency, effectiveness, impact and
sustainability. The intent is to incorporate lessons learned into the decision-making
process.
