Respectfully forwarded to the Office of the City Mayor, through the Office
of the City Administrator, both this City, the subject Ordinance No. 0224-20
SERIES of 2020, with the information that while the Ordinance is
timely in that it is an appropriate response to address the current Covid-19
situation within the city, however, this office finds some crucial defects which
goes into the validity of the ordinance as discussed hereunder, to wit:
1) It is too sweeping, and hence, vague
The ordinance failed to specifically mention the exact amount (estimate or
fixed) to be utilized in the purchase of Personal Protective Equipment (PPEs),
medical supplies and medicine,
Furthermore, while the Ordinance mentions the 5% Calamity Fund, it
failed to mention the specific source. Worthy to mention is that the 30o/o of the
Calamity Fund is for Quick Response and the 70olo is for Disaster Preparedness,
Mitigation and Rehabilitation as provided under the Joint Circular No. 2013-1
dated March 25, 2013 issued by the National Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management council, the Department of Budget and Management, and the
Department of Interior and Local Government.
It is undisputed that the disbursement of public firnds of an LGU requires
the authority of the SanEgunian by way of an ordinance, But the ordinance
authorizing the disbursement must fix the source of funcls and the arnount to be
disbursed by the executive department. Lest, it will give the executive
department unbridled discretion to use the calamity fund of the ciw or Davao.
A vague ordinance is frowrred upon. The Supreme Court, in the case of
People Vs. Nazario, 155 SCRA 187 held that as a rule, a statute or act may be
said to be vague when it lacks comprehensible standard that men of common
intelligence must necessarily guess at its meaning and differ as to its apptication.
It is repugnant to the Constitution in two respects: (1) it violate due process for
failure to accord persons, specially the parties targeted by it, fair notice of tlie
conduct to avoid; (2) and it leaves enforcers unbridled discretion in carrying out
its provisions and becomes an arbitrary flexing of the government muscle.

2) Prejudicial to public welfare
As stated above, the subject Ordinance leaves to the executive
department the unbridled discretion to spend the Local Dlsaster Risk Reduction
and Management Fund (LDRRMF). It has the pleasure to utilize the calamity fund
without limitation, which makes the ordinance prejudicial to public interest.
3) Contrary to law
Finally, record bears that there is no prior request made by the executive
department through the Local Finance Committee (LFC), for the utilization of the
calamity fund for the purchase of Personal Protective Equipment, etc., nor a
Certificate of Availability of Funds was issued therefor. There is also no prior
recommendation of the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Mitigation Council
(CDRRMC) on the matter.
The request by the executive department on the utilization of funds and
the certification on the availability of funds by the City Treasurer's Office and the
prior recommendation of the CDRRMC, are essential prerequisites to the issuance
of the subject ordinance.
In view thereof, this office recommends the veto of the subject ordinance.

SECTION 2. LEGAL BASIS - Republic Act No. 10121 also known as the "Philippine
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010," which was enacted will serue as
basis of this Ordinance.
SECTION 3. DECLARATION OF PRINCIPLES - Proclamation No. 922 issued by
the Office of the President declared a State of Public Health Emergency throughout the
Philippines due to Corona Virus Disease 2019 or Covid-19, while the City Government of
Davao in its Executive Order No. 10, SERIES of 2020, already declared the entire City of
Davao under a COMMUNITY QUARANINE.
SECTIOil 4. SOURCE OF FUNDS AND OBJECT OF EXPENDITURES. - SECTION
2L of the same Act provides for the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund
which can be used to support disaster risk management activities such as, but not Iimited
to, pre-disaster preparedness programs including training, purchasing Iife-saving rescue
equipment, supplies and medicines, for post-disaster activities, and for the payment of
premiums on calamity insurance, stated as follows:
SECTION 21 . Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund
(LDRRMF). - The present Local Calamity Fund shall hencefofth be known
as the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF).
Not Iess than five percent (5olo) of the estimated revenue ftom regular
sources shall be set aside as the LDRRMF to support disaster risk
management activities such os, but not limited to, pre-disaster
preparedness programs including training, purchasing life-saving rescue
equipment, supplies and medicines, for post-disaster activities, and for
the payment of premiums on calamity insurance.
SECTION 5. GENERAL PROVISIONS- The amounts herein provided shall be
used specifically for the PURCHASE OF PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE),
OTHER MEDICAL SUPPLIES AND MEDICINES and must conform to existing accounting and
auditing rules, regulations, policies and guidelines, as well as, other applicable laws,
ordinances and Presidential directives.
SECTION 6. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE - If, for any reason, any SECTION or
provision of this Ordinance is declared unconstitutional or invalid, other sections or
provisions hereof not affected by such declaration shall continue to be in full force and
